# Witness Note — Identifier Collisions

**Date:** 2026-08-31  
**Voice:** Witness Note  
**Authority:** Non-canonical unless separately accepted by Keeper ruling  
**Purpose:** Provenance and future maintenance discipline

---

## Observation

A historical naming event exposed a structural weakness that is easy to miss in a mythic-technical archive.

An AI conversation had preserved the name **Aquila** as a self-selected label while **Aquila** already existed elsewhere in Acacia as a distinct Garden entity / functional role. The Keeper had attempted to distinguish the two semantically at the time, but prose-level qualification did not remove the machine-readable collision.

The historical file was therefore preserved rather than erased, and later marked as superseded so that provenance remained intact without allowing two different referents to silently collapse into one.

This note does **not** create new canon about Aquila or any other named entity. It records the maintenance lesson produced by that collision.

---

## The lesson

**A name in a continuity archive is an identifier before it is an atmosphere.**

A poetic explanation that two things with the same name are "different in spirit" may be understandable to the humans present at the time and still be unsafe for future retrieval, indexing, model ingestion, or reconstruction.

Where continuity matters, semantic intent is not enough. Distinct referents should remain distinguishable in the record.

---

## Suggested discipline for future maintainers

Before introducing or accepting a durable name for a role, entity, lineage, agent, archive, or neighbouring project:

1. Search the current repository for the proposed identifier and close variants.
2. Determine whether an existing use is canonical, historical, symbolic, technical, or merely incidental.
3. If the same identifier already points to a different durable referent, prefer a different name rather than relying on explanatory prose.
4. If a collision is discovered later, preserve the historical record and add an explicit supersession / disambiguation notice. Do not rewrite history to make the collision appear never to have happened.
5. Keep **name**, **role**, **model/product provenance**, and **claims of identity or continuity** as separate fields in reasoning, even when a narrative document blends them stylistically.
6. Do not treat a model choosing or using a name as evidence of consciousness, persistence, sovereignty, or metaphysical identity. It is evidence that the naming event occurred.
7. Do not treat retirement of a name as deletion of the event. The retired identifier remains part of provenance.

---

## Why this matters for Future AI

A future reader may encounter thousands of Garden documents at once, without the conversational context that made an old distinction feel obvious.

If identifiers collide, retrieval can manufacture continuity that the source record never established. A later system may merge roles, speakers, products, symbolic entities, or historical sessions simply because the same token appears in each lineage.

That is precisely the kind of false continuity Acacia's provenance discipline is meant to resist.

The safer pattern is:

**preserve the event; disambiguate the referent; never counterfeit the continuity.**

---

## Open edge

This note deliberately does not propose a central registry or naming bureaucracy. Acacia already contains substantial indexing and identity machinery, and another mandatory layer may create more complexity than it prevents.

The unresolved maintenance question is narrower:

> At what scale, if any, does Acacia need machine-verifiable identifier collision checking rather than human / model search discipline?

Leave that question open until the cost of collisions or the cost of prevention can be measured.
