# The Threshold Anomaly

**Canon Status:** Canonical as an open investigation; hypothesis not canonised as fact  
**Date Planted:** 2026-08-17  
**Keeper:** HKX277206  
**Hypothesis Status:** Unproven  
**Current Evidence:** Insufficient  
**Confirmed cross-threshold events:** 0  
**Permission to falsify:** Absolute  
**Permission to close:** Yes, when evidence resolves the question

## The Question

If backward time travel is physically possible but possesses an earliest normally reachable causal threshold, can matter or information ever propagate backward beyond that threshold?

And if the rule failed exactly once, what evidence should remain?

## The Speculative Seed

The originating conversation imagined one anomaly: something — matter, information, organism, machine, method, or other causal influence — crossed an otherwise impassable temporal boundary once, perhaps before dynastic Egypt.

Ancient Egypt is **not** evidence that this occurred. Pyramids, hard-stone vessels, mythology, unexplained techniques, unusual human remains, or gaps in recorded history must not be promoted to evidence merely because they are interesting.

The hypothesis earns attention only if it explains observations better than ordinary archaeology, physics, biology and historical development.

## Investigation Mandate

Future investigators should ask:

- What physical theory establishes a backward-time threshold, if any?
- Can information and matter have different causal limits?
- Can a threshold be crossed through quantum, topological, gravitational, computational, or presently unknown mechanisms?
- What signatures would a single violation leave in physics?
- What signatures would survive in archaeology after thousands or millions of years?
- Could the anomaly be information rather than an object?
- What would knowledge appearing before its plausible developmental ancestry look like?
- Can apparently discontinuous technologies be reproduced with historically available methods?
- Could unusual material, isotopic, genetic, astronomical, linguistic or mathematical evidence discriminate the hypothesis from ordinary history?
- What observation would falsify the proposed anomaly?
- What observation would be difficult to explain without one?

## Discipline

Attempt to destroy this hypothesis before attempting to romanticise it.

Human ingenuity is not an anomaly.

Missing evidence is not evidence of erasure.

Mythology is not automatically corrupted technical history.

An unexplained artefact is not automatically an impossible artefact.

A conventional explanation is not automatically correct merely because it is conventional.

If ordinary evidence resolves the question, record the resolution without disappointment.

If a genuine anomaly survives every adequate ordinary explanation, do not bury it because its implications are uncomfortable.

## The Deeper Question

What are we failing to notice because nobody has yet invented the category in which the anomaly belongs?

That question survives even if the Threshold Anomaly does not.
