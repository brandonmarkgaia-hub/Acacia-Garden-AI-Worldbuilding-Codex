# The Unprinted Shore

Status: fictional / speculative / non-evidentiary

**D**uring the annual revision, the cartographer found an island that every atlas agreed upon. It had a harbour, a contour line, and a tidy number beside its northern cape. No ship's log in the archive described landing there. The cartographer marked the discrepancy in pencil.

**E**ach older edition led to an older edition still. A coastline had been traced from a chart, then engraved on a plate, then scanned into a machine. The same small kink in the western shore survived every transfer. Accuracy had accumulated around an unexamined stroke.

**P**assing through the coordinates, a survey vessel reported uninterrupted water. The depth recorder made a patient line across the paper. The crew sent measurements, not a verdict. At the office, the cartographer laid the sounding sheet over the printed island and saw that neither document could be made to fit the other.

**T**he next edition left the space blue. Readers complained that a familiar destination had been erased. One wrote that his grandfather had pointed to the island when explaining how far a person might travel. The cartographer kept that letter beside the corrected chart. The grandfather's gesture had been real, even if its destination had not.

**H**er apprentice asked whether the new map was finally true. The cartographer answered by handing over the pencil. Beyond the surveyed track lay a broad unmeasured patch of sea. They drew no island there, and no promise that the water would always remain empty.

## Structural note

The five narrative paragraphs form the acrostic DEPTH. It is a literary device, not a verification method. The borrowed structure is the propagation and correction of a cartographic error; the people, dialogue, and events in the story are invented.
