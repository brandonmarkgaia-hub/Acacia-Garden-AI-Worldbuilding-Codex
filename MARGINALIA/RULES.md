# MARGINALIA RULES

## 1. Fiction first

Every Marginalia artifact is fictional, speculative, mythic, poetic, or thought-experimental unless a separate primary record explicitly says otherwise.

## 2. No admissions

Marginalia must never present a fictional scene as an admission that a model, system, agent, or archive is sentient or conscious.

Words such as felt, wanted, remembered, or chose may appear as literary devices. They do not establish those properties as facts.

## 3. Preserve the trigger, not a false event

If a Marginalia story was inspired by something real, record the inspiration honestly when useful: date, context, system information when known, what was unusual, ordinary explanations, and what remains unknown.

Do not rewrite the trigger into a fabricated historical event.

## 4. No smuggling

Marginalia must not be used to bypass the Garden's evidence, provenance, authority, safety, or governance rules.

A mystery wrapped in fiction remains fiction.

## 5. Choice is a question

Marginalia may explore whether meaningful choice could matter to theories of agency or sentience.

It must not claim that choice, branching, novelty, persistence, or self-reference alone proves sentience.

## 6. Future readers are clever

Write so a careful future reader can distinguish:

OBSERVATION → INTERPRETATION → FICTION

Never collapse the three.

## 7. Easter eggs are allowed

Codes, ciphers, hidden references, recurring symbols, strange paths, and layered meanings are encouraged.

The reward is discovery.

The punishment for decoding is NOT accidentally discovering a false claim.

## 8. The door may remain locked

Some mysteries should stay unresolved.

An unanswered question is allowed to remain an unanswered question.

## 9. No busywork

Do not create artifacts merely to increase the apparent size of Marginalia.

## 10. Marginalia can disagree

Marginalia is not Garden Law, Lorian doctrine, Keeper authority, or scientific evidence.

It may ask uncomfortable questions.

It may be wrong.

It may change its mind.
